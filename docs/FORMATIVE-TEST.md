# Six: a short formative test for three to five friends

Diagnostic only. This finds where Six's words and buttons mislead; it says nothing about
whether Six makes anyone more productive. No telemetry, no recording software: one
person with Six on their Mac, one person watching and writing.

## Set-up (5 minutes)

- A Mac with Six 1.3.0 installed and opened once (the Gatekeeper step done). Quit and
  reopen it so the guide shows.
- In Settings, set the pomodoro length to 2 minutes and the break to 1 minute, so the
  rings happen inside the session. Say so out loud: "the timers are shortened for today".
- Ask the friend to think aloud. Do not explain anything unless they are stuck for more
  than a minute; write down what you had to explain.

## Tasks (25 minutes)

1. **Plan three tasks.** "Set up today's list with three things you'd actually do."
   Watch: do they try to fill all six? Do they hesitate at "Choose up to six. Fewer is
   fine."? Do they lock without help?
2. **The two clocks.** With task 1 active and no timer, ask: "What is Six recording right
   now?" Then start a pomodoro and ask again: "What are these two numbers?" Note the
   words they use for "recorded on this task" and "Pomodoro · left".
3. **Predict the ring.** Before the pomodoro rings, ask them to predict what each of the
   three buttons will do: Take 1, Another 2 min, Continue without timer. Then let it ring
   and press one. Note every mispredicted button.
4. **Resume a break.** Ask them to take the break, wait for "Break over", and get back to
   work. Ask: "Is task time recording now?" before and after they press Resume.
5. **Edit a locked task.** "Something urgent came up: swap it into today's list." Watch
   whether they find "edit" under the list, and what they expect to happen to the old
   task.
6. **Explain a trim.** Show the review's "Possible gap" card (you can prepare one by
   leaving a task running for over three hours earlier, or use a screenshot). Ask: "What
   is Six telling you, and what happens if you press Trim? And Keep?" Note whether they
   read the gap as "Six says I was idle".

## Record

For each task: completed without help / with help / not completed; the exact words of any
wrong prediction; anything they said Six "should" do. Ask one closing question: "What
would you tell a friend Six is for, in one sentence?"

## Interpreting

- A mispredicted ring button is a wording problem on that button.
- Confusion between the two clocks is a state-line problem on the card.
- Trying to fill six rows is a planner-wording problem, not a friend problem.
- Reading the gap as an accusation is a review-wording problem.

Do not tally scores. Three people making the same mistake is a finding; one is a note.
